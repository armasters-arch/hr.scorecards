📊 High Performer Rate — Deep Dive

34 of 100 new hires reached high performer status (≥4.5/5 at 1-year review), beating the 30% target by 4 percentage points.

  • Employee referrals drove the highest HP rate at 54% — more than double the agency average
  • Career site applicants were second at 38%, outperforming LinkedIn (25%) significantly
  • HP rate correlates strongly with faster time to productivity — HP hires averaged 5.8 weeks vs 9.1 weeks for non-HP
  • Manager satisfaction scores are 0.8 points higher on average for HP new hires
  • None of the 34 HP hires have separated; all-cause attrition is entirely in the non-HP population
✕ close

📉 Retention Rate — Deep Dive

82 of 100 hires were still employed at the 1-year mark. The 18% attrition rate is 3pp above the 15% benchmark.

  • Q1 (0–90 days): Only 2 departures. Both were voluntary resignations citing role misalignment — sourced via agency
  • Q2 (90–180 days): 4 more departures. The 6-month mark is the highest single-risk window historically
  • Q3 (180–270 days): 5 departures. This cohort's attrition accelerated here — worth investigating manager relationships
  • Q4 (270–365 days): 7 departures. Voluntary in all cases. Exit surveys cite compensation and growth opportunities
  • All 18 departures were non-HP performers. Zero HP attrition in year 1 — a strong signal for sourcing quality
✕ close

⏱ Time to Productivity — Deep Dive

Average 7.6 weeks across the cohort to reach a manager-rated ≥4/5 on core role tasks. This is 0.6 weeks slower than FY2023.

  • Employee referrals: 5.5 wks — fastest ramp. Prior relationship with culture reduces friction
  • Career site: 6.5 wks — above average. Self-selected candidates tend to research the role deeply
  • Campus/university: 7.5 wks — expected for early-career. Strong trajectory post-ramp
  • LinkedIn/job boards: 8.5 wks — broad applicant pool increases fit variance
  • Agency: 10 wks — slowest and highest cost. Warrants review of agency briefing quality
  • Ramp time has a 0.74 correlation with 90-day performance score across the cohort
✕ close

⭐ Manager Satisfaction — Deep Dive

4.1/5 overall on the post-90-day manager survey — up from 3.8 last year. Ramp speed remains the weakest dimension.

  • Skills match (4.3): Strongest dimension. Hiring bar and job descriptions appear well-calibrated
  • Culture fit (4.4): Highest rated. Values-based interview additions in Q1 2024 appear to be working
  • Ramp speed (3.7): Weakest area. Managers cite insufficient pre-boarding materials and delayed system access as primary blockers
  • Overall quality (4.1): In line with benchmark. Target is 4.3 by end of FY2025
  • Managers who completed structured 30-day check-ins rated new hire ramp speed 0.6 points higher on average
✕ close

📋 30-Day Review — Cohort Analysis

Cohort average: 3.1/5. The 30-day review captures early onboarding quality and initial role fit signal.

  • Score distribution: 12 hires scored ≥4.0 (early standouts), 61 scored 3.0–3.9 (on track), 27 scored below 3.0 (at-risk)
  • The 27 at-risk hires at 30 days: 19 recovered to ≥3.5 by 90 days; 8 remained below 3.5 and ultimately separated
  • Strongest departments at 30 days: Engineering (3.6 avg) and People (3.8 avg)
  • Weakest departments: Sales (2.9 avg) — historically consistent; Sales ramp takes longer due to pipeline build time
  • Key 30-day predictor: hires who completed pre-boarding modules scored 0.5 points higher on average
✕ close

📋 60-Day Review — Cohort Analysis

Cohort average: 3.5/5. The 60-day mark shows the steepest average score improvement (+0.4) of any interval.

  • 93% of hires who scored ≥3.5 at 60 days went on to complete the full year — strong retention predictor
  • Score improvement from 30→60 days averaged +0.4 pts. Hires who improved less than +0.2 had 3× higher attrition risk
  • The 60-day check-in is where manager coaching impact is most visible — structured 1:1 cadence correlated with +0.3 score lift
  • Remote hires scored 0.2 pts lower at 60 days on average, but caught up by 90 days when intentional touchpoints were in place
✕ close

📋 90-Day Review — Cohort Analysis

Cohort average: 3.9/5. The 90-day score is the strongest early predictor of 1-year performance and retention.

  • Correlation between 90-day and 1-year score: r=0.81 — the single best predictive checkpoint
  • Hires scoring ≥4.0 at 90 days had 96% 1-year retention vs 71% for those scoring below 3.5
  • 47 hires reached ≥4.0 at 90 days; 31 of those 47 were ultimately classified as high performers
  • Recommendation: consider using 90-day score as a formal gate for confirming permanent employment status
✕ close

📋 1-Year Review — Cohort Analysis

Cohort average: 4.2/5. 82 of 100 hires reached the 1-year review; 18 had separated before the milestone.

  • Of 82 who completed the review: 34 rated HP (≥4.5), 41 rated solid performer (3.5–4.4), 7 rated below expectations (<3.5)
  • The 7 below-expectations hires at 1 year represent candidates for performance management in Year 2
  • Average 1-year score by source: Referral (4.6), Career site (4.3), Campus (4.2), LinkedIn (3.9), Agency (3.5)
  • 3 hires received promotions at or before the 1-year mark — all sourced via employee referral
✕ close
